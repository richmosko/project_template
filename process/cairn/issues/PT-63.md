#### Finding: the fonts activate an accessibility risk the spec explicitly names

Not a defect against my ruling — **a gap in my ruling.** I scoped PT-57 to colour
and pre-decided PT-63 as files-and-subsets, and neither mentioned this.

`body` now sets `var(--font-sans)` — Merriweather, a **serif** — and everything
inherits it. Enumerated, **eleven selectors at 11–12px now render in the serif**:

`.chip` (11px) · `.drawer-field label` (11px) · `.major-tab-open` (11px) ·
`.filters label` (12px) · `.view-state-btn` (12px) · `table.issue-list th` (12px) ·
`.connection-state` (12px) · `.comment-meta` (12px) · `.drawer-progress` (12px) ·
`.record-readonly-note` (12px) · `.pr-link/.file-link/.parent-link` (12px)

The design spec's Accessibility section names **exactly these two cases** —
badge/chip labels and small form labels — as *"a real, new accessibility-adjacent
risk, not inherited from the old spec"*, and recommends either bumping them to
`text-sm`+ or scoping `--font-sans` out of the smallest components. The board's
chips are **11px**, below even the 12px the spec was describing.

**This is a design call, not an architecture one**, so options rather than a
ruling:

- **(a) Scope the smallest components to `--font-heading`** (Space Grotesk — the
  system's humanist sans) on chips and small labels. Cheapest, and arguably *more*
  on-spec: face is by role, and these are labels rather than prose. ~11 selectors.
- **(b) Bump 11–12px text to 14px** (the spec's `text-sm`). Follows the spec's
  first suggestion literally, but materially changes board density — a visual
  change well beyond this issue.
- **(c) Accept and record**, on the grounds that this is a localhost dev tool.

**My recommendation: (a), as a follow-up issue, not a change to this PR.** PT-63's
AC is delivery and delivery is correct and complete. The risk arrived *with* the
fonts, so it belongs to whoever owns the visual system — ux-designer's spec says
it, and the call is Mosko's, not mine.

Approve stands independent of which option wins.
