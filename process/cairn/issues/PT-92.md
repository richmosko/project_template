## Problem

PT-85 introduced three dedicated categorical tokens for the throughput chart —
`--chart-flow-opened`, `--chart-flow-closed`, `--chart-flow-wip` — with interim values taken
from the dataviz skill's validated categorical palette, chosen by implementation-lead under two
constraints only: mutually distinguishable in every theme variant, and not coinciding with a
`--chart-role-*` hue used by the token/cost chart on the same dashboard page. No design review.

## Ask

ux-designer ratifies or replaces the three values against the design system (docs/DESIGN,
tokens.css preset). Names and the per-variant distinguishability guard are the contract; values
may change. If replaced, update app.css and every variants.css entry, rebuild dist, and re-run the
guard.

## Acceptance criteria

1. A decision recorded on this issue: ratified as-is, or replaced with the new values and why.
2. If replaced: the guard test green in every variant, light and dark; dist rebuilt and committed.
3. Browser-visibility pass on the throughput chart in at least two variants (light + dark).

### @architect — 2026-09-08

**Gate 1 ruling — PT-92. Three lines is necessary and not sufficient: coincident values reproduce the exact defect Mosko reported, and the ruling has to close that too.**

**The measurement that shapes the feature.** Live `/api/flow`, 17 points: **all three equal 0/17**, but **at least two equal on 3/17 (~18 %)** — `2026-09-07` is `opened=3, closed=3` right now. Plain lines at a coincident x **overlay exactly**, so the later-drawn series hides the earlier one. That is the same "only shows if wip > opened > closed" failure the board comment reports for bars, just at 18 % of points instead of most of them. Separately, `wip` is **0 on 16 of 17 points**, so the WIP line lies along the x-axis rule where it is easy to mistake for the axis itself.

**Requirement, binding; the mechanism is ux-designer's call.** At **every** x, including where two or three series share a value, each series must remain individually identifiable. Two treatments satisfy this and both are design-legitimate: **per-series point markers** (a mark drawn at each datum survives an exact path overlay) or **per-series dash patterns** (a solid path under a dashed one still reads). Draw-order plus opacity does **not** satisfy it — at an exact overlay the lower series contributes nothing an observer can name — and a vertical offset is rejected outright as a lie about the value. ux-designer picks the treatment and the WIP-on-axis remedy; I am fixing only the property.

**(a) Seam.** `IssueFlowChart.svelte` L216–251. Keep the `BarChart` container — it owns the scales, axes, tooltip, the Day/Week and milestone controls, and the `yDomain` that already covers all three plotted values (L104–106) — and replace the grouped `Bars` at L229 with a line mark per series inside the same marks snippet. **This is proven, not proposed:** the WIP `Spline` at L237 already draws on that band scale today, so three Splines on the same scale is the existing arrangement generalised, not a new rendering path. Do not switch the container to `LineChart`; that is what would put the controls and the Show-table path at risk, and those stay untouched.

**(b) ux-designer's entry under PT-82.** From its own worktree: `git pull --rebase origin feature/pt-92-chart-flow-tokens-lines`, commit by pathspec — its decision comment on this issue, any `app.css` / `variants.css` token changes, and the `docs/DESIGN` note — then `git push origin HEAD:<feature-branch>` fast-forward and report the sha. **Before qa's red**, since the guard re-runs against the final values. ux-designer does **not** touch `dashboard/dist` (implementation-lead's) or `IssueFlowChart.svelte`.

**(c) Red tests — qa.**
1. The existing `test_flow_series_distinctness.py` re-run against the final three values, in **every** variant, light and dark (AC2). Mutation: set two of the three to the same value.

3. Legend and caption strings name all three series. Mutation: drop one from the legend list.
4. **Coincidence, the one this feature exists for:** on a fixture point where two series share a value, both still resolve to their own mark. Testable at the config level (both series present in the mark set with distinct treatment); the *rendered* proof is the lead's leg, and the ruling says so rather than pretending a source scan settles it.
