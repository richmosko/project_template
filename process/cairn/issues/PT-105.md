### @architect — 2026-09-08

**Ruling (gate 1).** Measured first, on this machine, with the module's own helpers against a real fake receiver — elapsed from just before the `--session-ended` spawn (an upper bound on the daemon's timer start) to the in-window operation completing, n=5 per row:

| in-window deadline | at rest | 8-way CPU burn | 8-way spawn storm | budget | worst-case margin |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| `GraceWindowFlushContentTests`, POST (`grace = 0.6`) | 0.091–0.100 | 0.100–0.107 | 0.115–0.134 | 0.6 | 0.466 s (4.5×) |
| `LastSessionSelfStopTests`, immediate `--status` (`GRACE = 0.4`) | 0.165–0.169 | — | 0.208–0.217 | 0.4 | 0.183 s (1.9×) |

**The issue targets the safer of the two.** The 0.4 s deadline in `test_one_session_start_end_exits_after_grace_period_with_a_flush` is 2.4× tighter and degrades faster under load. Both get fixed here.

**(1) Values.** Introduce `INSIDE_WINDOW_GRACE = 2.0` and use it for exactly the two deadlines above (`grace = 0.6` → it; `LastSessionSelfStopTests.GRACE` → it **only** for that one test). `GRACE = 0.4` stays for the wait-*past*-the-window tests — `test_two_starts_one_end_still_running` sleeps `GRACE + 1.0`, where a bigger window is pure wall cost and buys no safety. One constant cannot serve both directions; that conflation is the real defect.

**(2) The characterisation is a margin assertion, and it is the deterministic red.** In each of the two tests: `t0 = time.monotonic()` before the `--session-ended` spawn, and after the in-window operation returns, assert `grace - (time.monotonic() - t0) >= 1.0` with a message naming the measurement. Today that fails on the **first run, at rest**: remaining is ≤ 0.509 s (0.6 test) and ≈ 0.231 s (0.4 test) against a 1.0 s floor — measured above, no "run until it flakes". After the fix, remaining ≈ 1.79 s, i.e. the guard tolerates an 8× slowdown before firing. The floor is 4.6× the worst elapsed I measured under load; do not set it where a plausible load spike trips it, because a false-firing guard is the same disease.

**(3) Sibling, same shape, different lever.** `test_a_start_during_the_grace_window_cancels_the_exit` (local `grace = 1.0`) spends `grace * 0.3` sleeping plus a spawn before the in-window session start — ≈ 0.4 s of a 1.0 s window, 2.5×, tighter than the test this issue names. Fix it by shrinking the work, not growing the window: replace `time.sleep(grace * 0.3)` with a fixed `time.sleep(0.1)` (margin → ≈ 4×, wall cost **−0.2 s**), keep `grace = 1.0` and the `grace + 1.0` post-deadline wait unchanged. No margin assertion needed there; the cancel is proved by the log line.

**(4) Cost, stated because it is real.** This file is the parallel suite's critical path (20.8 s of a 25.2 s wall in PT-107's gate-4 run; PT-98 tuned it 18.9 → 16 s). The two raises cost ≈ +1.4 s and +1.6 s, the sleep fix returns 0.2 s: net ≈ **+2.8 s on every parallel full run**. I am ruling that a red gate costs more than 2.8 s. Documented fallback, no re-ruling needed: `INSIDE_WINDOW_GRACE = 1.0` with a 0.5 s floor still holds ≥ 4.6× margin (measured) at half the cost — that is the floor, do not go below it.

**(5) Attribution is `(unmeasured)` and must not be claimed.** PT-107's gate-4 parallel failure text was never captured — my miss. The measurements above say the 0.6 s window it was blamed on had 4.5× margin even under a spawn storm, so this issue is margin hygiene, **not** a proven fix for that failure. If the module fails again at a gate, capture the assertion text before touching another constant. `_wait_for_status_not_running(timeout=grace + 4.0)` is a second deadline in the same test that scales with `grace`; leave it, but include the measured elapsed in its failure message so the next failure names which deadline expired.
