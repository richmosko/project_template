### @team-lead — 2026-09-09

Mosko (2026-09-09, after reloading): "the chart color does not match the chart-color selection (though the color does change). Selecting Pink shows shades of brown. Selecting Amber shows shades of teal. Selecting Cyan shows shades of purple."

**Cause (from PT-118's own derivation, read off variants.json):** the rotation is Δh = variant `chart-1` hue − yellow's `chart-1` hue (98.1°), applied to families whose base hues are unrelated to yellow (counter 205°, opened 16.9°, closed 135.2°, wip 200.8°). The families keep their spread but never land on the chosen hue: Pink (Δh ≈ 252°) puts the counter family near 97° (brown); Cyan puts it near 300° (purple); even the default "yellow" renders the token chart teal. The derivation is an isometry, not an anchor — mathematically consistent, perceptually wrong.

**Ask:** the family's anchor hue equals the selected variant's `chart-1` hue. Counter family (one hue, four L/C steps — ordinal): all four take the selected hue, keeping their L/C steps; Pink → pink stacks. Flow family (three lines that must stay ≥60° apart, PT-92): ux-designer decides which series carries the selected hue (recommend `opened`, the first legend item) and the other two keep their offsets from it; the coincidence mechanism (dash + graduated markers) is hue-independent so PT-92's guards still bind. Consequence to state plainly: the default `yellow` will render yellow-anchored charts instead of today's teal/red — that is the point (the control names the colour the chart wears); the "yellow byte-identical" guard from PT-118 is replaced by "the default renders the default's hue". Gamut mapping and the contrast/distinctness sweep from PT-118 stay as-is (hue fixed, L/C nudged where a hue fails a floor — yellow-family hues near L .6 on white may need it). Acceptance: switch to Pink/Amber/Cyan, reload, both charts' rendered hues are within ±3° of the selected `chart-1` hue on their anchor series (elementFromPoint-sampled), two variants × light/dark; sweep green across all 24 × 2.

### @ux-designer — 2026-09-09

**Revised for Mosko's steer (ux-designer): use the vendored `--chart-1..5` values verbatim. No rotation, no gamut-mapping, no new hue math — PT-120's anchor draft above is superseded, not layered on.**

**Mechanism simplification, named because it changes the whole shape of the work:** since flow/counter would just *be* specific `--chart-N` steps, the cleanest implementation is a `var()` alias — `--chart-flow-opened: var(--chart-1)` etc. — declared **once**, in `app.css`, no per-variant block, no generator changes, no isometry/gamut math at all. `--chart-1..5` already varies correctly per variant (that mechanism is untouched, already ships); aliasing rides it for free. Architect/implementation-lead's call on mechanism, but it's the obvious one — flag before anyone builds the rotation machinery this steer makes unnecessary.

**Mapping — flow (opened/closed/wip → chart-1/3/5, widest available spread):** `opened=--chart-1`, `closed=--chart-3`, `wip=--chart-5`, in legend/z-order — simplest, most explainable assignment, using the two extremes plus the midpoint for the largest achievable *lightness* separation within a 5-step ramp.

**Mapping — counter (4 ordinal steps → chart-2/3/4/5, chart-1 skipped):** `output=--chart-2` (lightest of the four used) … `input=--chart-5` (darkest), preserving today's light=output/dark=input direction. **Chart-1 deliberately unused here** — it's the step already on record (2026-08-28) as failing 2:1 against white; skipping it for counter, which has no pattern-based backup the way flow's lines do, avoids handing a guaranteed-visible bar fill the worst step when a spare is available. Flow doesn't have this luxury (needs 3 of the 5 for any spread) and gets chart-1 anyway — accepted, not dodged, below.

**PT-92's ≥60° pairwise distinctness guard is not "sometimes" failed by this — it fails by construction, always.** `--chart-1..5`'s own hue spread is ~30-40° total per variant (yellow: 61.9°-98.1°) — under any 3-step subset, pairwise ΔH tops out around 20-25°, never close to 60°. This isn't a per-variant measurement question the way contrast is; it's the defining property of a single-hue ordinal ramp. **Recommend formally retiring `test_flow_series_distinctness.py`'s pairwise/role-collision assertions**, same precedented action as PT-85 retiring the ordinal-ramp test when its assumption stopped holding — not weakening the threshold, retiring the check with recorded rationale. What actually carries the categorical read now is PT-92's **pattern** layer (solid/dashed/dotted stroke + graduated markers), which is hue-independent by design and unaffected by this steer — that mechanism is why this is survivable at all, not a coincidence.

**PT-83's 2:1 contrast floor: record the exception, don't nudge — per the ask.** Chart-1/chart-2 failing 2:1-on-white in **light mode only** is already on record (2026-08-28, `--chart-1..5` left untouched because nothing consumed them then). Now `opened` (chart-1) inherits it directly, for whichever variants' chart-1 fails. Proposal: keep the mechanical sweep, but where a mapped step fails, **append to a recorded exception ledger** (variant, mode, token, measured ratio) instead of nudging the value or blocking the build — the values must stay exact, so a failure is a fact about the preset, not a bug to fix here.

**Yellow default's appearance — larger swing than PT-120's draft:** `opened/closed/wip` become 3 shades of the same gold ramp (not PT-92's red/green/teal), and counter becomes gold shades too (not the muted cyan family) — every chart, every default view, changes. Confirming this is the intent, since it's larger than what the anchor draft would have done.

**Role family: unaffected, stays fixed** — nothing about this steer touches `--chart-role-*`; the PT-118/PT-69 identity argument still applies untouched.

**Answering the gate-1 brief's question directly: can two flow series ever land on the same color?** No, structurally, not just today — `--chart-1..5` is monotonic in lightness by construction (that's what makes it a valid *ordinal* ramp; a non-monotonic step would already fail the existing ramp validator), so `--chart-1 ≠ --chart-3 ≠ --chart-5` holds for every variant × mode with no exception to check for. The three flow lines are always three distinct colors; they're just closer together in hue/lightness than PT-92's dedicated palette made them, which is the guard-retirement point above, not a coincidence risk.

### @architect — 2026-09-09

**Gate 1 ruling (architect).** Measured against all 24 vendored ramps (23 in `variants.json` + `yellow` in app.css). ux-designer's design-2 landed while I was measuring; **I adopt their mechanism and their mapping**, and the numbers below are why each choice survives — plus two corrections that are mine.

**What a shadcn chart ramp actually is.** Five steps, `L` ≈ 0.90 → 0.47, hue drifting slightly along the ramp (median max intra-ramp spread **6.4°**, max 45.4°). **All 24 variants have identical light and dark ramps** — mode-invariance is a property of the vendored data, not an assumption, so nothing needs per-mode generation.

**Seam: ux-designer's `var()` alias, adopted — it is better than the generator copy I was about to rule.** `--chart-flow-opened: var(--chart-1)` and friends, declared **once** in `app.css`, no per-variant emission at all. The variant block already redefines `--chart-1..5`, so the alias resolves to the selected ramp by cascade. Exactness then holds **by construction rather than by assertion**, there is nothing to regenerate when a preset changes, and PT-118's rotation + gamut-mapper are deleted rather than bypassed.

**Mapping, adopted as ux-designer specified it:** flow `opened=chart-1, closed=chart-3, wip=chart-5`; counter `input=chart-5 … output=chart-2`, `chart-1` unused there. My own sweep independently picked the same flow set — of the five triples I measured, (1,3,5) is the only one clearing ΔE 0.06 with margin in every variant (min **0.1195**; (2,3,5) 0.0596, (2,4,5) 0.0620, (2,3,4) 0.0512).

**Correction 1 — the counter ramp has its own measured cost, and it must be recorded, not assumed away.** Contiguous `chart-5/4/3/2` leaves **four variants with adjacent steps below ΔE 0.06**: violet **0.0512**, indigo 0.0583, blue 0.0584, rose 0.0596. (Skipping `chart-3` — 5/4/2/1 — gives 0 such variants, at the price of `chart-1`'s contrast miss, which ux-designer has on record from 2026-08-28 as the reason to avoid it.) I take their trade, because it is the same trade the whole feature makes: **exact values outrank the floors**. But then the adjacency floor cannot stay a gate while the contrast floor becomes an observation — both retire together, and both get a table.

**Correction 2 — the sRGB gamut gate must retire too, and PT-118's hard-fail cannot ship with exactness.** **62 of 120 vendored values are outside sRGB** (worst overshoot 0.064 linear-light, median 0.0163) — a P3-wide palette by construction, `yellow` included. A hard fail would refuse the vendored data itself. It becomes an observation like the others; the browser gamut-maps, as it already does today.

**PT-92's ≥60° hue rule: retired for flow, with the number.** Minimum hue gap between any three steps of one ramp is **0.2°** (violet); **all 48 variant×mode combinations fail**. Replaced by ΔE ≥ 0.06 as the measured property and PT-92's hue-independent coincidence mechanism (dash + graduated markers) as the mechanism. **No variant makes two flow series identical** — I checked all 24, and ux-designer's monotonic-lightness argument is why it cannot happen.

**Observation table (mine to hand over; belongs on this issue and in the spec).** Contrast vs both `--card`s, two-model: `chart-1` misses 2:1 in **24/24** light, `chart-5` in **11** dark, `chart-4` in **7** dark, `chart-2` in **2** light. Counter adjacency below ΔE 0.06: violet, indigo, blue, rose (values above). Gamut: 62/120 outside sRGB.

**ux-designer's open question, answered with the mechanism.** `index.html`'s inline `<script id="cairn-theme-bootstrap">` runs in `<head>`, before the module script and before first paint — **no flash**. But it sets `data-cairn-chart` **only when the preference differs from the default** (line 55), so under `yellow` **no attribute is ever set** and `:root` is the permanent render path. Under the alias seam this is automatic and correct: `:root`'s `--chart-1..5` *are* yellow's ramp, so the aliases resolve to it with no duplicated literals anywhere. That also removes the "app.css contains template-only hues" hazard entirely — after this change app.css contains no standalone flow/counter colour literals at all.

**Guards (qa; red at gate 2).**
1. Source: the seven family tokens are `var(--chart-N)` aliases per the mapping, declared once in `app.css`, with **no** `--chart-flow-*` / `--chart-counter-*` literals left in `app.css` or any generated `variants.css`.
2. Resolution: for every variant × mode, the computed value of each family token equals that variant's mapped `--chart-N` — asserted through the cascade (a CSS-level resolution test or the lead's browser leg), not by re-deriving the value.
3. Flow separation: min pairwise ΔE ≥ 0.06 across 24 × 2, and no two flow tokens equal in any variant.
4. Counter adjacency, **as an observation**: the four sub-floor variants above are asserted **by name and value**, so a change in the vendored data shows up as a diff rather than silence. Same for the contrast and gamut tables.
5. Deletion: PT-118's rotation and gamut-mapper symbols and their tests no longer exist — assert absence, or a bypassed derivation keeps passing its own suite.
6. The default renders the default's hue: `yellow`'s aliases resolve to yellow's ramp; PT-118's "byte-identical to the old values" guard is deleted, and its deletion asserted.

**Writers.** ux-designer → spec + the observation tables. implementation-lead → `app.css` aliases, `gen_variants.py` deletions, `variants.css` ×3, dist. qa → the six guards.
